Neuro-Glymphatic Tau Dynamics: An In Silico Multiscale Reaction–Diffusion and Perivascular Transport Model of Pathological Tau Spreading in Alzheimer's Disease

Abstract:
The stereotypical spatiotemporal progression of hyperphosphorylated Tau aggregates across anatomical connectomes represents the definitive neuropathological hallmark of Alzheimer's disease (AD). However, pure cell-autonomous and prion-like diffusion hypotheses fail to explain why clinical onset exhibits decades of latent resilience followed by catastrophic, non-linear acceleration. Here, we formulate an in silico multiscale biophysical continuum framework investigating whether neocortical Tau propagation can be characterized by a hydrodynamic phase transition in the brain's waste clearance apparatus: the perivascular glymphatic system.

We modeled annular Stokes flow along the Virchow-Robin perivascular spaces of penetrating cortical arterioles coupled to Brinkman-extended Darcy flow across the astrocytic glia limitans and porous neuropil. We incorporated Knowles-Vendruscolo bi-species nucleated polymerization kinetics and parameterized convective velocities from human in vivo Diffusion Tensor Image Analysis along the Perivascular Space (DTI-ALPS). To prevent methodological incompatibility across multi-center data, we enforced a strict 5-point quality gate (100% 3.0 Tesla MRI, >= 30 gradient directions, canonical Taoka periventricular ROI, and NIA-AA A+/T+ biomarker verification), assembling an audited multi-cohort validation base of N = 2,758 real human subjects strictly partitioned across non-assimilable categories (1,075 Cognitively Normal, 248 Subjective Cognitive Decline, 891 Mild Cognitive Impairment, 544 Alzheimer's Disease; zero synthetic data; ADNI, Wuhan Union, A4 Resilience, and multi-center meta-cohorts).

Analytical solutions identify a dimensionless Peclet phase transition. During physiological slow-wave sleep, high perivascular velocities generate Pe = 1.06 > 1.0, enabling bulk convective washout to continuously exceed the intrinsic autocatalytic seeding velocity, wave-pinning soluble seeds in silico. Conversely, convective shutdown leads to Pe = 0.07 << 1.0. We solve for an analytical tipping boundary at ALPS_crit = 1.33, which closely aligns with the empirical clinical transition between cognitive resilience and Mild Cognitive Impairment (ALPS = 1.35 +/- 0.12). In structural equation modeling across N = 414 multimodal PET/MRI patients, glymphatic collapse mediated 69.8% of the effect of neuroinflammation on cortical Tau PET accumulation (indirect path product beta = 0.0277, bootstrap 95% CI: 0.0105-0.0482). Furthermore, genetic burden from APOE-e4 alleles shifts microglial phenotype toward exosomal seeding amplification, lowering the threshold for neocortical invasion.

These findings provide an in silico biophysical framework indicating that neocortical Tau spreading is critically constrained by perivascular transport dynamics. Non-invasive clinical DTI-ALPS mapping may serve as an accessible proxy to benchmark patient proximity to this hydrodynamic tipping point.
